\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================

We introduced DPRI, the first pre-training dataset privacy risk estimator
grounded in a formal bound on membership inference advantage.
Across seven datasets in four domains, DPRI predicts MIA vulnerability
with $R^2 = $\TODO{X.XX} using five distributional features that require
no model training.
Three findings challenge established practices: dataset structure
dominates model architecture as a risk driver; widely used benchmarks
are structural outliers that inflate reported attack success; and DP
protection efficacy depends on data geometry, not just on $\varepsilon$.

These results suggest a shift in how the community measures and reports
membership inference risk.
We call for benchmark diversity in MIA evaluations, for dataset-level
risk assessment as a standard pre-training step, and for data-adaptive
DP budgeting in privacy-sensitive deployments.

The DPRI tool is available at \url{https://github.com/ShuyaFeng/dataset_privacy}.
