\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================

We introduced DPRI, a pre-training dataset privacy risk estimator
grounded in a formal bound on membership inference advantage. Across 31
datasets in five domains, DPRI ranks MIA vulnerability at an unbiased
cross-validated Spearman correlation of $0.61$ from distributional
features alone, a moderate rather than precise prediction, which is
itself a finding: privacy risk is significantly shaped by data geometry
yet determined by no single pre-training statistic. Two further results
challenge established practice. The benchmarks that anchor a decade of
membership inference research are geometric outliers unrepresentative of
deployed data, and among realistic models dataset structure governs risk
more than model architecture, so an attack number reported on a single
benchmark conflates three distinct sources of variation.

We therefore call for benchmark diversity in MIA evaluations and for
dataset-level risk assessment as a standard pre-training step, with
direct measurement remaining the final arbiter for high-stakes releases.
The most consequential follow-ups are a pre-training proxy for label
complexity, which our failure analysis identifies as the main missing
signal, and testing whether DPRI-calibrated differential-privacy budgets
deliver more uniform protection. The DPRI tool is provided as an
anonymized artifact and will be released as open source upon
publication.
