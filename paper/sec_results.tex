\subsection{Finding 1: Data Structure Dominates Model Architecture}
\label{sec:finding1}

To decompose variance, we compute:
\begin{align}
  V_{\text{data}}  &= \mathrm{Var}(\mathrm{AUC} \mid \text{dataset varies, model fixed}), \\
  V_{\text{model}} &= \mathrm{Var}(\mathrm{AUC} \mid \text{model varies, dataset fixed}).
\end{align}
Averaged across all configurations, dataset choice explains
\textbf{\TODO{XX}\%} of total AUC variance, while model architecture
explains \textbf{\TODO{XX}\%} (Figure~\ref{fig:variance_decomp}).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \dprifig{../results/regression/variance_decomp.pdf}{0.85\linewidth}
  \caption{Variance decomposition of MIA AUC across all 63 configurations.}
  \label{fig:variance_decomp}
\end{figure}

This finding challenges a tacit assumption in the MIA literature: that
attack strength and model architecture are the primary levers of privacy
risk.
Our result implies that a practitioner who trains a privacy-preserving
model on a high-DPRI dataset will achieve lower protection than one who
trains a standard model on a low-DPRI dataset, even if the former uses
DP-SGD.
