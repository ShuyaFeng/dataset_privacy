\begin{abstract}
% Write abstract LAST — after all results are in.
% Key elements: 1 sentence problem, 3 findings with numbers, 1 sentence implication.
%
Membership inference, attribute inference, differential privacy, and
machine unlearning have been studied for a decade, yet they all treat
privacy leakage as a property of the model. We ask a more basic
question. Why do some datasets leak far more than others under the
same model and the same attack? We show that much of the answer lies in the geometric structure of the
training data itself. We introduce the Dataset Privacy Risk Index
(DPRI), a risk score computed entirely from the raw data distribution. It
combines sample uniqueness, local density, outlier structure, feature
entropy, and class separability, and it requires no trained model, no
shadow models, and no attack. We prove that the advantage of an optimal
membership inference adversary is upper-bounded by a function of these
pre-training quantities, giving a formal account of why data geometry
shapes privacy risk.

Across a cross-domain study spanning tabular, medical, recommendation, and
mobility data, we find that membership inference risk varies dramatically
across datasets. It is governed more by data structure than by the model,
and far more than by the choice of attack. We use DPRI to revisit
standard practice in privacy evaluation, with implications for the design
of membership inference benchmarks and the calibration of differential
privacy. Our results reframe dataset privacy risk as a quantity that can be
anticipated before training rather than measured after deployment, and we
release DPRI as an open-source tool for pre-training dataset auditing.
\end{abstract}
