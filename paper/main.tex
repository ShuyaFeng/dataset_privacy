\begin{abstract}
% Write abstract LAST — after all results are in.
% Key elements: 1 sentence problem, 3 findings with numbers, 1 sentence implication.
%
Membership inference, attribute inference, differential privacy, and
machine unlearning have been studied for a decade, yet they all treat
privacy leakage as a property of the model. We ask a more basic
question. Why do some datasets leak far more than others under the
same model and the same attack? We show that a significant part of the answer lies in the geometric
structure of the training data itself. We introduce the Dataset Privacy
Risk Index (DPRI), a risk score computed entirely from the raw data
distribution. It combines sample uniqueness, local density, outlier
structure, feature entropy, class separability, and data dimensionality,
and it requires no trained model, no shadow models, and no attack. We prove
that the advantage of an optimal membership inference adversary is
upper-bounded by a function of these pre-training quantities, giving a
formal account of why data geometry shapes privacy risk.

Across 31 datasets spanning medical, image, network, recommendation, and
tabular domains, DPRI predicts the ranking of membership inference risk with
an unbiased cross-validated Spearman correlation of 0.61. The prediction is
moderate rather than precise. Data geometry significantly shapes risk but
does not fully determine it, and we find that no single statistic suffices.
We further find that the benchmarks anchoring a decade of MIA research are
geometric outliers that do not represent typical deployed data, and that
dataset structure influences risk comparably to model architecture and more
than the choice of attack. Our results reframe dataset privacy risk as a
property that can be partially anticipated before training rather than only
measured after deployment, and we release DPRI as an open-source tool for
pre-training dataset auditing.
\end{abstract}
