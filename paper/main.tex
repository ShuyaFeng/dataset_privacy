\begin{abstract}
% Write abstract LAST — after all results are in.
% Key elements: 1 sentence problem, 3 findings with numbers, 1 sentence implication.
%
Membership inference, attribute inference, differential privacy, and
machine unlearning have been studied for a decade, yet they all treat
privacy leakage as a property of the \emph{model}. We ask a more basic
question: why do some datasets leak far more than others under the
\emph{same} model and the \emph{same} attack?

We show that membership inference is largely a symptom of the geometric
structure of the training data itself. We introduce the \emph{Dataset
Privacy Risk Index} (DPRI), a risk score computed entirely from the raw
data distribution---sample uniqueness, local density, outlier structure,
feature entropy, and class separability---without training a single model,
without shadow models, and without mounting any attack. We prove that the
advantage of an optimal membership inference adversary is upper-bounded by
a function of these pre-training quantities, explaining formally why data
geometry, rather than model architecture, should govern privacy risk.

Using DPRI, we establish three findings with direct consequences for
security practice: (i)~dataset structure, not model architecture, is the
dominant driver of membership inference risk; (ii)~the benchmarks that have
anchored a decade of MIA research are structural outliers that overstate
the risk of the data practitioners actually deploy; and (iii)~the
protection delivered by a fixed differential privacy budget depends on
dataset geometry, so identical $\varepsilon$ values yield systematically
unequal protection. Together, these results turn privacy risk into a
quantity that can be predicted before training rather than measured after
deployment. We release DPRI as an open-source tool for pre-training dataset
auditing.
\end{abstract}
